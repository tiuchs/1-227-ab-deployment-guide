Welcome

📖 A Letter to Our Families ▸

Dear Families,

We hope this Deployment Smart Guide provides useful information, resources, and points of contact to support you while your Soldier serves in Europe. As our Soldiers prepare to deploy, we recognize that families are also preparing for an important mission at home. Our priority is ensuring you have the support needed to navigate this transition successfully.

To support our families throughout the deployment, the battalion has established a Rear Detachment and remains committed to maintaining responsive communication and assistance. Our Soldier and Family Readiness Group (SFRG) volunteers are prepared to help connect families with the appropriate resources and support services as needs arise.

This guide is intended to provide timely, accurate information and serve as an initial resource for common questions and challenges. It does not replace the knowledge, experience, and support available through your company SFRG leadership and members. We will also publish a monthly newsletter emailed out as a reliable channel for official information and updates.

In the event of an unexpected issue or concern, your first point of contact should be your company Family Readiness Group Advisor. They can help identify the appropriate resource and connect you with the support needed to address your situation.

Your primary mission in the coming months is caring for your family during your Soldier’s absence. We also ask that you continue to look after one another as members of our Army family. Thank you for your continued support of your Soldier, your family, the battalion, and our fellow Army families.

👪 Soldier & Family Readiness Groups (SFRG) ▸

An SFRG is a volunteer organization made up of all the Soldiers, civilians and family members of a particular unit. The SFRG is designed to provide mutual social and emotional support, outreach services, and information to its members. SFRGs welcome those who have an interest in the unit (retirees, parents, aunts, uncles, grandparents, brothers, sisters, cousins, and significant others of single Soldiers), need its services, and/or are willing to help the SFRG meet its goals. Families that can cope with — and in many instances actually enjoy — Army life are more likely to contribute to the community and allow their Soldier to focus on their mission. The best help for families comes from peers as they learn how to handle various aspects of Army life. SFRGs are part of a larger Army effort to help families adapt to the challenges of Army life.

The assistance that SFRGs provide is the kind of help we all need and try to get every day. They are the primary source of information flow to families during the deployment, offer opportunities to meet other family members in your unit, help resource issues, and offer an opportunity to meet and talk through things with other people who are experiencing similar situations.

To get involved with your SFRG, please start by contacting your Battalion level SFRG Advisor or your CFRR.

🚨 Significant Emergencies & Red Cross Notification ▸

To notify your Soldier of an emergency while deployed, the Red Cross must be contacted. The Red Cross will verify the emergency (e.g., hospital, doctor, funeral home, etc.) to begin the notification procedures. The Chain of Command / CFRR is always available to assist you through this process.

  1. Call your Fort Hood Red Cross Agency during business hours at (254) 287-0400 or the 24-hour emergency line at (877) 272-7337.
  2. Please ensure that the following is available:
    • Soldier’s name, rank, Social Security Number (SSN), and Date of Birth (DOB)
    • Soldier’s unit and deployed address
    • Emergency situation and relationship to Soldier
    • Name, address, telephone number of the hospital, doctor, funeral home, etc. that can verify the emergency
    • Local point of contact and phone number of that person in the area of the situation
  3. The Red Cross will then pass the information to the unit. The Commander will have the final authority approving someone to return on leave under emergency conditions because of a Red Cross message.
  4. If leave is approved, transportation arrangements will be made by the military to ensure the Soldier is able to return to the place where his or her presence is required.

Fort Hood Red Cross Office: Building 288, located on the corner of Battalion Ave. and 37th Street, or on the first floor at Darnall Army Medical Center. Open Monday – Friday, 8:00am – 4:00pm.
